@dialog
Marta: ¿Has comprado el regalo para tu madre? | Have you bought the present for your mother?
Marek: Sí, lo compré ayer. Es un libro de cocina. | Yes, I bought it yesterday. It's a cookbook.
Marta: ¿Y cuándo se lo vas a dar? | And when are you going to give it to her?
Marek: Se lo doy el domingo, en su cumpleaños. | I'll give it to her on Sunday, on her birthday.
Marta: ¿Y las flores? | And the flowers?
Marek: Las compro el domingo por la mañana. ¿Me ayudas a elegirlas? | I'll buy them on Sunday morning. Will you help me choose them?
Marta: Claro. Te llamo el sábado y lo hablamos. | Sure. I'll call you on Saturday and we'll talk about it.

@grammar
## Direct object pronouns (whom? what?)
|| me | me
|| te | you
|| lo / la | him, it (masc.) / her, it (fem.)
|| nos | us
|| os | you (all)
|| los / las | them (masc. / fem.)
**¿El libro? Lo compré ayer.** — The book? I bought it yesterday. (el libro → lo — “it” follows the gender of the noun)

## Indirect object pronouns (to whom?)
|| me | (to) me
|| te | (to) you
|| le | (to) him, her, you (formal)
|| nos | (to) us
|| os | (to) you (all)
|| les | (to) them, you (formal plural)
**Le doy un regalo a mi madre.** — I give my mother a present. (Spaniards often say both: le … a mi madre.)

## Two pronouns at once: le/les → se
First “to whom”, then “what”. **le / les + lo / la / los / las → se lo / se la…**:
**Le doy el libro → Se lo doy.** (never “le lo”!)

## Where the pronoun goes
Unlike English, the pronoun usually comes **before** the verb.
- Before a conjugated verb: **Lo compré.**
- With an infinitive or gerund — before or attached: **Se lo voy a dar = Voy a dárselo.**
- With an affirmative command — attached: **¡Dámelo!**

@culture
In Spain (especially in Madrid) you'll also hear **le** instead of **lo** for men: **Le vi ayer** instead of **Lo vi ayer**. This is called **leísmo** — the Royal Academy accepts it when talking about a man.
