@grammar
## Ir a + infinitive — the simplest future
Just like English “going to”: **ir** (voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van) + **a** + infinitive:
- **Voy a visitar a mis padres.** — I'm going to visit my parents.
- **¿Qué vas a hacer mañana?** — What are you going to do tomorrow?
- **Va a llover.** — It's going to rain.

## Tener que — to have to
**tengo que / tienes que / tiene que…** + infinitive: **Tengo que trabajar.**

## Querer / poder + infinitive
**Quiero ir. ¿Puedes venir?** Notice the stem changes: quiero, puedo.

## The personal “a”
When the object of a verb is a **person**, Spanish puts **a** before it (English has nothing here): **Visito a mis padres. Invito a Marta.** But: **Visito Madrid** (a place — no “a”).

## Venir and traer
- **venir**: vengo, vienes, viene, venimos, venís, vienen
- **traer**: traigo, traes, trae, traemos, traéis, traen

@culture
When invited to a Spanish home it's fine to arrive ten or fifteen minutes late — turning up exactly on time for a private get-together can even surprise people. For work and official appointments, though, you arrive on time.
