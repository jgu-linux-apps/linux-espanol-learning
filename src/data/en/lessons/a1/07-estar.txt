## The verb estar — present tense
|| yo | estoy
|| tú | estás
|| él / ella / usted | está
|| nosotros/as | estamos
|| vosotros/as | estáis
|| ellos / ellas / ustedes | están

## Ser or estar? The key rule
English has one verb “to be”; Spanish has two.
**SER** — what/who something is, lasting features:
- identity, job, nationality, origin: **Soy ingeniero. Es de Málaga.**
- personality and looks: **Es alto. Es simpática.**
- dates and the time: **Son las tres. Hoy es lunes.**
**ESTAR** — how/where something is, states:
- how you feel and temporary states: **Estoy cansado. Está enfermo.**
- location: **El hotel está cerca de la playa. Estoy en casa.**
- **estar de vacaciones** — to be on holiday.

## How to choose quickly? Two questions
1. Am I saying **who / what / what kind** someone is by nature (job, nationality, feature, time, what it's made of)? → **SER**
2. Am I saying **where** someone is or **how they are right now** (mood, state, location)? → **ESTAR**
- Trick: **estar** sounds like “**state**” and “**stay**”: states and where things stay. **Ser** is the essence of a thing.
- Careful: **ser** is not used for location! You say **Estoy en Londres**, not “Soy en Londres”. Origin is ser: **Soy de Londres** (where I'm from).

## The most common mistakes
|| ✗ Soy cansado. | ✔ Estoy cansado. (tiredness is a state)
|| ✗ Estoy ingeniero. | ✔ Soy ingeniero. (a job is identity)
|| ✗ Somos en casa. | ✔ Estamos en casa. (location)
|| ✗ Estamos ingleses. | ✔ Somos ingleses. (nationality)
|| ✗ El hotel es cerca. | ✔ El hotel está cerca. (location)
- Remember: **somos** = we are (ser), **estamos** = we are / we are located (estar).

## The same adjective, a different meaning
|| Es aburrido. | He's boring (a boring person).
|| Está aburrido. | He's bored (right now).
|| Es listo. | He's clever.
|| Está listo. | He's ready.

@culture
In Spain many shops and offices close for the siesta, roughly from 2 to 5 pm, especially in smaller towns. You'll often see the sign **cerrado** on the door at that time.
