@grammar
## Pretérito perfecto: haber + participle
It's built like the English present perfect (“I have spoken”):
|| yo | he hablado
|| tú | has hablado
|| él/ella/usted | ha hablado
|| nosotros/as | hemos hablado
|| vosotros/as | habéis hablado
|| ellos/ellas/ustedes | han hablado
Participle: **-ar → -ado** (hablado), **-er/-ir → -ido** (comido, vivido). It never changes with the person!

## When to use it (in Spain)
In Spain the pretérito perfecto describes the past within a period that **is still going on** — even where English uses the simple past:
- **hoy, esta mañana, esta semana, este año** — **Hoy he trabajado mucho.** (I worked a lot today.)
- life experiences: **¿Has estado en Madrid?** (more at A2)
- with **ya** (already) and **todavía no** (not yet).

## Irregular participles
|| hacer | hecho
|| decir | dicho
|| ver | visto
|| escribir | escrito
|| volver | vuelto
|| poner | puesto
|| abrir | abierto
|| romper | roto
|| morir | muerto
Also: **ir → ido**, **ser → sido**.

## Word order
Nothing goes between **haber** and the participle: **Ya he comido. No he comido.** Pronouns go before haber: **Me he levantado.**

@culture
A typical difference: a Spaniard says **Hoy he comido paella**, a Mexican rather **Hoy comí paella**. Learning the Spanish of Spain, use the perfecto for “today” and “this week”.
