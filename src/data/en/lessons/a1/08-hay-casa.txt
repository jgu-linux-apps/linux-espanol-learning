@grammar
## Hay — there is / there are
**Hay** is one form for singular and plural. Use it with an indefinite article, a number or no article at all:
- **Hay un balcón.** — There's a balcony.
- **Hay dos dormitorios.** — There are two bedrooms.
- **¿Hay supermercados cerca?** — Are there any supermarkets nearby?

## Está / están — where a particular thing is
For a particular, known thing (with **el, la**, a name, **mi**, etc.) use **estar**:
- **El baño está al lado del dormitorio.** — The bathroom is next to the bedroom.
- **Mis llaves están encima de la mesa.** — My keys are on the table.

## Contractions: al and del
**a + el = al**, **de + el = del**: **al lado del sofá** (next to the sofa). With **la** there is no contraction: **al lado de la cama**.

## Piso
In Spain **el piso** is a flat in an apartment block, and also a floor: **Vivo en el tercer piso.** (I live on the third floor.)

@culture
Most Spaniards in towns live in apartment blocks (**pisos**), not houses. On the ground floor there's often a bar or a shop, and older buildings have a concierge (**el portero**).
