@grammar
## Age: tener + años
In Spanish you don't “are” an age, you **have** years: **Tengo 30 años** — I'm 30 (literally “I have 30 years”). Never say “soy 30”!

## The verb tener (to have) — irregular
|| yo | tengo
|| tú | tienes
|| él / ella / usted | tiene
|| nosotros/as | tenemos
|| vosotros/as | tenéis
|| ellos / ellas / ustedes | tienen

## Numbers 16–19
They are a shortened “ten and six”: **dieciséis** (diez y seis), **diecisiete**, **dieciocho**, **diecinueve**. Watch the accent on **dieciséis**.

## Uno, un, una
Before a masculine noun **uno** becomes **un**: **un año** (one year), but **una hermana** (one sister). When counting, though, you say “uno, dos, tres…”.

@culture
Spanish mobile numbers usually start with 6 or 7 and have 9 digits. Spaniards often say them in pairs or threes: “seiscientos quince, cero tres…” — to begin with, digit by digit is fine.
