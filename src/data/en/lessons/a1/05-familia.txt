@grammar
## Possessives (before the noun)
|| mi / mis | my
|| tu / tus | your (informal)
|| su / sus | his, her, its, your (formal); their
|| nuestro, nuestra / nuestros, nuestras | our
|| vuestro, vuestra / vuestros, vuestras | your (plural)
- **mi, tu, su** only change for number: **mi hermano**, **mis hermanos**.
- **nuestro, vuestro** also change for gender: **nuestra casa**, **vuestros padres**.
- **su** can mean “his”, “her”, “their” or the polite “your” — the context tells you which.

## Masculine plural for mixed groups
**Los padres** are parents, **los hijos** are children (sons and daughters), **los hermanos** are brothers and sisters, **los abuelos** are grandparents. The masculine plural covers a mixed group.

## Este, esta, estos, estas
**este chico** (this boy), **esta mujer** (this woman), **estos padres**, **estas fotos**.

@culture
Spaniards have **two surnames**: the first from the father, the second from the mother, e.g. Federico García Lorca. Women don't change their surname when they marry.
