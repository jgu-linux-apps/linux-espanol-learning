@goals
Say where you're from and what your nationality is
Learn the verb ser in the present tense
Learn the subject pronouns
@dialog
Lucía: ¿De dónde eres, Marek? | Where are you from, Marek?
Marek: Soy de Polonia, de Rumia. Es una ciudad cerca de Gdańsk. | I'm from Poland, from Rumia. It's a town near Gdańsk.
Lucía: ¡Qué interesante! Entonces eres polaco. | How interesting! So you're Polish.
Marek: Sí, soy polaco. ¿Y tú? ¿Eres española? | Yes, I'm Polish. And you? Are you Spanish?
Lucía: Sí, soy española, de Valencia. | Yes, I'm Spanish, from Valencia.
Marek: ¿Y tu amigo Marc? | And your friend Marc?
Lucía: Marc es de Barcelona. Habla español y catalán. | Marc is from Barcelona. He speaks Spanish and Catalan.

@grammar
## Subject pronouns
|| yo | I
|| tú | you (informal)
|| él / ella / usted | he / she / you (formal)
|| nosotros / nosotras | we (masculine / feminine)
|| vosotros / vosotras | you all (masculine / feminine)
|| ellos / ellas / ustedes | they (masc. / fem.) / you all (formal)
**Usted** and **ustedes** (the polite “you”) take the 3rd-person form of the verb. In Spain you say **vosotros** to a group of friends, and **ustedes** is the polite form.
The pronoun is usually left out, because the verb ending already shows who is acting: **Soy inglés** (no need for “yo soy”).

## The verb ser (to be) — present tense
|| yo | soy
|| tú | eres
|| él / ella / usted | es
|| nosotros/as | somos
|| vosotros/as | sois
|| ellos / ellas / ustedes | son
Ser is used, among other things, for origin (**ser de** + place), nationality, profession and identity.

## Nationalities: masculine and feminine
- Ending in **-o** → feminine in **-a**: polaco → polaca, italiano → italiana.
- Ending in a consonant → add **-a**: español → española, alemán → alemana (the accent disappears!), inglés → inglesa.
Unlike in English, nationalities and languages are written with a **small letter**: español, inglés. Countries take a capital: España, Inglaterra.

@culture
In Spain, besides Spanish (castellano), Catalan, Galician and Basque (euskera) are official languages in their regions. That's why Spaniards often call their language **castellano**.
