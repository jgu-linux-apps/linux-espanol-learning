@grammar
## What is he like? — ser + adjective
For lasting features (looks, personality) use **ser**: **Es alto. Es simpática.**
The question: **¿Cómo es?** — What is he/she/it like?

## Adjective agreement
The adjective agrees with the noun in gender and number and usually comes **after** the noun:
|| | masculine | feminine
|| singular | un chico alto | una chica alta
|| plural | unos chicos altos | unas chicas altas
- Adjectives in **-e** and most in a consonant have one form: **inteligente, joven, azul, verde**.
- Adjectives in **-or** add **-a**: trabajador → trabajadora.
- Plural: **-s** after a vowel (altos), **-es** after a consonant (azules, jóvenes).

## Tener for describing
Eyes and hair are something you **have**: **Tiene los ojos verdes. Tiene el pelo rubio / largo / corto.**

@culture
In Spain **guapo** doesn't only mean “good-looking” — people also say “¡Qué guapo!” about a nice thing, like a new jacket. And **moreno** can also mean someone with a suntan.
