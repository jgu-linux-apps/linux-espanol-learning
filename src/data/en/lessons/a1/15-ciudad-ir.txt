@goals
Say where you're going and how (ir a, en)
Ask for and give directions
@dialog
Marek: Perdone, ¿para ir al Museo del Prado? | Excuse me, how do I get to the Prado Museum?
Señora: Está un poco lejos. Puede ir en metro o en autobús. | It's a bit far. You can go by underground or by bus.
Marek: ¿Y andando? | And on foot?
Señora: Andando son veinte minutos. Siga todo recto por esta calle y gire a la derecha en la plaza. | On foot it's twenty minutes. Go straight along this street and turn right at the square.
Marek: ¿Todo recto y luego a la derecha? | Straight on and then right?
Señora: Eso es. El museo está a la izquierda, enfrente del parque. | That's it. The museum is on the left, opposite the park.
Marek: Muchas gracias. | Thank you very much.

@grammar
## The verb ir (to go) — irregular
|| yo | voy
|| tú | vas
|| él/ella/usted | va
|| nosotros/as | vamos
|| vosotros/as | vais
|| ellos/ellas/ustedes | van
- **ir a + place**: **Voy a Madrid. Voy al banco** (a + el = al).
- **ir en + means of transport** (“by”): **en coche, en tren, en metro**. But on foot: **a pie / andando**.

## Giving directions (usted form)
- **Siga todo recto.** — Go straight on.
- **Gire a la derecha / a la izquierda.** — Turn right / left.
- **Cruce la calle.** — Cross the street.
- **Coja la primera / segunda calle.** — Take the first / second street.
To a friend (tú): **sigue, gira, cruza, coge**.

## Coger in Spain
In Spain **coger** (to take, to catch) is a completely neutral word: **coger el autobús**. In Latin America it's better avoided — there people say **tomar el autobús**.

@culture
The Madrid underground is one of the largest in Europe. Spain also has fast **AVE** trains — from Madrid to Seville they take about 2.5 hours.
