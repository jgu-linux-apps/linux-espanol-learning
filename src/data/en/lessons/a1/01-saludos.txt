@grammar
## Greetings during the day
In Spain **buenos días** is used until lunch (about 2–3 pm), then **buenas tardes** until it gets dark, and in the evening **buenas noches** — which means both “good evening” and “good night”.

## The verb llamarse
Spaniards don't say “my name is”, they say “I call myself”. The pronoun **me / te / se** goes before the verb:
|| yo | me llamo | my name is
|| tú | te llamas | your name is
|| él / ella / usted | se llama | his/her/your (formal) name is

## Question and exclamation marks
In Spanish a question or an exclamation also has a mark **at the start**, upside down: **¿Cómo te llamas?**, **¡Hola!**. In the app you type them with the ¿ and ¡ buttons.

## Encantado or encantada?
A man says **encantado**, a woman **encantada** — the adjective agrees with the person speaking. You can also simply say **mucho gusto**.

@culture
In Spain friends (and often people you've just met) greet each other with **dos besos** — two kisses on the cheeks, starting with the right one. Men usually shake hands with each other.
