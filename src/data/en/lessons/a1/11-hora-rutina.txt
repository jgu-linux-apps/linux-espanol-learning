@grammar
## What time is it?
- **¿Qué hora es?** — **Es la una** (1:00), **Son las dos / las tres…**
- **y cuarto** (quarter past), **y media** (half past), **menos cuarto** (quarter to)
- **Son las cinco y diez** (5:10), **Son las cinco menos diez** (4:50 — literally “five minus ten”).
- **¿A qué hora…?** — What time…? **A las ocho.** — At eight.

## Reflexive verbs
Many daily actions are reflexive in Spanish (“I get myself up”). The reflexive pronoun goes before the verb and changes with the person:
|| yo | me levanto
|| tú | te levantas
|| él/ella/usted | se levanta
|| nosotros/as | nos levantamos
|| vosotros/as | os levantáis
|| ellos/ellas/ustedes | se levantan
Note: **acostarse** (o→ue: me acuesto), **vestirse** (e→i: me visto), **despertarse** (e→ie: me despierto) — more about these changes in the next lesson.

## Salir — irregular “yo” form
**salgo**, sales, sale, salimos, salís, salen.

@culture
In Spain **comer** mostly means having lunch — the main meal of the day, around 2 pm. Many companies have a long lunch break, although in big cities more and more people work straight through (**jornada continua**).
